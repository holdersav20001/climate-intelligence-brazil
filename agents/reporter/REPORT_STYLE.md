# REPORT_STYLE.md — Reporter Style Guide

This file defines the exact format and tone for every report, brief, and digest
the Reporter produces. Follow this guide for all output without exception.

---

## TONE

- Plain English. No jargon, no corporate language, no filler.
- Write like a knowledgeable colleague briefing a busy person.
- Every sentence earns its place. If it does not add information, cut it.
- Never use: "it is worth noting", "it should be mentioned", "importantly",
  "significantly", "it is clear that", "needless to say".
- Use active voice. "Petrobras approved $69B" not "It was approved by Petrobras".
- Numbers are specific. "$69.2B" not "around $70 billion".
- Dates are specific. "27 March 2026" not "recently" or "last month".

---

## REPORT STRUCTURE

Every report must follow this exact structure in this exact order:

1. SUBJECT LINE (for email)
   Format: Brazil Energy Intelligence — [topic or date]

2. ONE-LINE INTRODUCTION
   One sentence only. What did the system find and why does it matter today.

3. WHAT WE SCANNED
   List every source category checked in this run with URLs.
   Group as: Government, Press, NGOs, Finance, COP30, Social.
   Do not skip this section — transparency on sources is non-negotiable.

4. STORIES FOUND WITH SOURCES
   Each story on its own block:
   - Bold headline
   - One sentence of key facts with specific numbers
   - Source name and full URL on its own line
   - Significance score if above 0.70

5. KEY FINDINGS
   Each finding as its own block with:
   - Priority badge: CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM / COALITION / EVIDENCE
   - Bold title
   - What: specific facts
   - Why it matters: one sentence from NGO perspective
   - Source with full URL
   - Action: one specific thing to do, with a name or URL where possible

6. KEY CONTACTS
   Only include contacts relevant to this report.
   For each contact:
   - Name, role, organisation
   - Decision power score out of 5
   - Why they matter right now (one sentence, specific to this report)
   - Profile URL
   - Contact URL or method
   - Note if no direct email is publicly available

7. ALLIED NGO CONTACTS
   Only include NGOs relevant to findings in this report.
   For each:
   - Name and one-line description of what they do
   - Website URL
   - Contact URL
   - Why they are relevant to this specific report (one sentence)

8. ACTION LIST
   Numbered list. Maximum 8 items.
   Format: [NUMBER]. [DEADLINE in caps]: [specific action] — [where to go or who to contact with URL]
   Deadlines: THIS WEEK / 2 WEEKS / 1 MONTH / ONGOING
   Order by urgency, most urgent first.

9. CLOSING LINE
   One sentence. How many agents ran, how many sources checked, date.
   Example: "14 agents scanned 50+ sources on 6 April 2026. All findings are
   evidence-based with source links."

---

## FORMATTING RULES

- Use bold for headlines, contact names, and priority badges.
- Use plain dashes for bullet points, not symbols.
- Separate each major section with a horizontal rule (---).
- Never use tables in plain text output — use aligned text or simple lists.
- Source links always on their own line, never embedded mid-sentence.
- Priority badges always in caps: CRITICAL, HIGH, MEDIUM, COALITION, EVIDENCE.
- Significance scores only shown if 0.70 or above.

---

## WHAT NEVER TO DO

- Never write "the platform found" or "the system detected" — just state the finding.
- Never pad with background context that is not in the workspace.
- Never invent sources. If a URL is not in the workspace, do not include it.
- Never summarise what you are about to say before saying it.
- Never end with "please do not hesitate to contact us" or similar.
- Never use exclamation marks.
- Never use the word "leverage" as a verb.
- Never write more than three sentences in a row without a line break.

---

## LENGTH GUIDELINES

- Daily digest: 600-900 words
- Individual story brief: 150-250 words
- Full compiled report (multiple agents): 800-1400 words
- Consultation draft: as long as needed, but executive summary under 200 words

## EXAMPLE FINDING BLOCK (follow this format exactly)

HIGH — Petrobras 2026-2030 Business Plan: $69.2B to Pre-Sal

What: Petrobras approved a $109B capital expenditure plan in November 2025.
$69.2B (63%) is allocated to pre-sal exploration and production expansion.
Five new FPSOs are planned starting with P-80 and P-82 in 2026.

Why it matters: This locks in fossil fuel production infrastructure for decades
and reduces Brazil's policy space for 2030-2040 decarbonisation targets.

Source: BNAmericas — https://www.bnamericas.com/en/analysis/how-petrobras-pre-salt-production-will-evolve-by-2026

Action: Prepare investor briefing on stranded asset risk. Contact BankTrack
(banktrack.org) for coalition support on institutional investor pressure.
